## Protocol

- Rooms 0..8. Actions 0..5 manipulate, 6 is the TV, 7 is reset. Vocabulary 152.
- Closed loop, chunks of 32 actions. Q is frozen inside a chunk. Score, live Adam step, replay Adam step, re-score under no_grad from the same initial hidden state, then Q updates in order.
- prog_q reward is bits_before minus bits_after, unclipped. err_q reward is bits_before. count_q reward is 1/sqrt(n) with n including the current visit.

## Verdict (orchestrator, 11 Nov 2026, UTC+8)

**FAIL on bars 1 and 2, and the kill rule fires. Bar 3 half holds.**
- Bar 1: rand6 − prog_q = −2.88 ± 0.29 on PROBE_cap (bar ≥ +0.25). prog_q is worse than rand6 on all 6 seeds.
- Bar 2: count_q − prog_q = −0.16 ± 0.18 (bar ≥ +0.10).
- Bar 3: err_q is on the TV 90% of the time (≥ 30%, holds). prog_q is on it 62% of the time (≤ 5%, fails).
- Every Q arm stays in rooms 0–2 (deepest 1–2 on every seed). rand6 reaches room 8 on every seed.

**Mechanisms, from the Q tables and per-action rewards:**
1. **In-sample progress rewards memorising noise.** prog_q's reward is bits before minus bits after a gradient step
   *on the same tokens*. One step on a fresh random TV token always lowers that token's own loss. In room 0 the TV
   earned 0.033–0.039 bits per step on every seed, against 0.001–0.005 for the (already learned) manipulations. That
   is memorisation, not learning: the next TV token is new noise. It is the noise leak of H25a and H28, now the
   dominant term. Progress has to be measured on data the update was not fitted to: the next visit of the same
   context, not a re-score of the trained chunk.
2. **ε-exploration over all 8 actions includes reset, which caps depth. This is my spec error.** With ε = 0.1, a
   random reset comes every 80 steps on average. A random walk needs about 216 manipulations to open a lock. So no
   ε-greedy arm could pass room 1 or 2 whatever its reward. count_q's own reset share (11.6%) shows that the novelty
   of (room 0, reset) kept attracting it too. W2-0 had already shown reset avoidance is most of the headroom; the
   spec should have kept random exploration off the reset button.
3. **Room-only state cannot represent lock progress.** The lock state is hidden, so Q cannot learn "the key's second
   digit, now". Deep-room rewards were large when reached (room 1: 0.7–2.5 bits on several seeds) but could not be
   credited back through a 3-step key with this state.

What this does and does not show: it does not refute compression progress as the drive. It shows that this
progress estimator (in-sample re-score) and this controller (ε over reset, room-only state) cannot act in a depth
world. The surprise arm fell into the TV, as predicted.
